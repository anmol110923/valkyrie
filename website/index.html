Spaces

A space for every part of your life

Work, study and personal each get their own color, pinned tabs and tabs. Switch with ⌃1–⌃9 and stop mixing contexts.

Pinned & folders

Pinned tabs that stay put

Drag tabs into Pinned, group them in nested folders, and keep your favorite sites as an icon grid. Closing a pinned tab never unpins it.

Command bar

Everything is one ⌘K away

Jump to any tab, pinned item, bookmark or page from your history, or run commands like split view, new space and theme.

Split view

Two pages, side by side

Docs next to your notes, or a meeting next to the agenda. Right-click a tab, or press ⌃⇧=.

Extensions

The whole Chrome Web Store

Click “Add to Chrome” on any extension. uBlock, Dark Reader, 1Password, Grammarly and more just work, and they update themselves.

Shields

Ads and trackers, gone

Built-in blocking based on EasyList and EasyPrivacy, with nothing to install. The shield in the URL bar shows what was stopped on each page.

Passwords

A password manager that stays home

Valkyrie offers to save logins and fills them in one click. Passwords are encrypted with your system keychain and never synced to a server.

Light & dark

Beautiful in both

Follow your system or choose Light or Dark. Press ⇧⌘L to flip anytime. Instrument Serif and Inter make every page easy to read.

Tidy by default

Tabs that clean up after themselves

Tabs you haven’t looked at in 12 hours are archived automatically, and you can reopen any of them later. Pinned tabs and favorites are never touched.

Digital wellbeing

Your attention,
accounted for.

Every new tab is your dashboard: a year-long heatmap of your screen time, an hour-by-weekday “when you browse” map, streaks, your daily goal, and your most-used sites and pages. Press ⌘Y for the full view. The Wellbeing page adds hourly and weekly charts and a searchable list of every page you visited and how long you stayed.

  • Counts real attention only. Idle tabs don't count, playing media does.

Install

  1. Download the .dmg for your Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel).
  2. Open it and drag Valkyrie into the Applications folder.
  3. Open Valkyrie from Applications or Spotlight.
  1. Download the .exe (most PCs need x64).
  2. Run the installer, choose a folder, and finish. A desktop shortcut is created.
  3. Open Valkyrie from the Start menu or desktop.

First launch warnings

Valkyrie isn't signed with a paid Apple or Microsoft certificate yet, so your computer asks you to confirm the first time. This happens only once.

macOS: “Valkyrie can't be verified”

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.

macOS: “Valkyrie is damaged and can't be opened”

Open Terminal, paste this, press Return, then open Valkyrie again:

xattr -cr /Applications/Valkyrie.app
Windows: “Windows protected your PC”

Click More info, then Run anyway.

Import from Chrome (or Brave, Arc, Edge, Vivaldi)

  1. Open Settings with ⌘, or the gear at the bottom of the sidebar.
  2. Under Import, choose the browser and the profile.
  3. Tick what you want: bookmarks, history, passwords, cookies (to stay signed in) and extensions.
  4. Click Import. On a Mac, click Allow when macOS asks to use “Chrome Safe Storage” from your Keychain. This is how Chrome protects your passwords.

Passwords and cookies can be imported on macOS only for now. Bookmarks, history and extensions import on every platform.

Make it your default browser

  1. In Valkyrie, open Settings → Make Valkyrie default.
  2. When macOS or Windows asks, choose Valkyrie.
  3. To open PDFs in Valkyrie on a Mac, right-click any PDF in Finder → Get Info → Open with: Valkyrie → Change All.

Sidebar, spaces & tabs

  • Spaces: click the icons at the bottom of the sidebar, or press ⌃1–⌃9. Use + to create one. Right-click a space to rename it, change its icon or color, or delete it.
  • Favorites: drag a tab to the icon grid at the top, or right-click it → Add to Favorites.
  • Pinned: drag tabs into the pinned area, or press ⇧⌘D. Use the folder button to create folders. Double-click anything to rename it.
  • Today: your regular tabs. Clear closes them all. Unused tabs are archived after 12 hours, and you can change this in Settings → Tabs.
  • Command bar: ⌘K searches tabs, pinned items, bookmarks and history, and runs commands.
  • Split view: right-click a tab → Open in Split View, or press ⌃⇧=. Press ⌃⇧- to exit.
  • ⌘S collapses the sidebar. Move the mouse to the left edge to peek.
  • Mute a tab with its speaker icon, and rename it with a double-click. ⇧⌘C copies the URL.

Wellbeing

Open a new tab for the dashboard (year heatmap, hour heatmap, streaks, top sites and pages). Click Today in the sidebar or press ⌘Y for the full Wellbeing page. Set a daily goal in Settings → Wellbeing.

  • A second counts only when a tab is in front of you and you're active, or when it's playing audio. After 60 seconds without keyboard or mouse input, counting stops.
  • Day and Week views show screen time by hour or day, your top sites, and a comparison with the previous day.
  • Footprint lists every page with the time you opened it and how long you stayed. You can search it.
  • Uncheck Track my activity to pause tracking. You can delete a day, a week or everything.

Passwords

  • Click a login field and a small Valkyrie menu shows your saved accounts for that site.
  • After you sign in somewhere new, Valkyrie asks whether to Save the password, Never save it for that site, or skip it for now.
  • View, show and delete passwords in Settings → Saved passwords.
  • Passwords are encrypted with your macOS Keychain or Windows DPAPI. They never leave your device.

Extensions & ad blocking

  • Click the puzzle icon in the sidebar, or go to Extensions → Chrome Web Store. Then click Add to Chrome.
  • Extension buttons and popups appear in the sidebar. Extensions update themselves every few hours.
  • Ad and tracker blocking is always on. The orange shield in the URL bar shows how many requests were blocked on the page.
  • Most popular extensions work. A few that rely on rare Chrome features (side panels, keyboard commands) may not yet.

Where your data lives

Everything is stored on your computer:

  • macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/Valkyrie/
  • Windows: %APPDATA%\Valkyrie\

Inside you'll find wellbeing/ (one JSON file per day), bookmarks.json, history.db and passwords.json (encrypted). Delete the folder to reset Valkyrie completely.

Build from source

Valkyrie is built with Electron (Chromium), so you need Node.js.

git clone https://github.com/anmol110923/valkyrie.git
cd valkyrie
npm install
npm start          # run from source
npm run dist       # build Valkyrie.app
npm run release    # build the .dmg and .exe installers

VALKYRIE_DEBUG=1 npm start logs blocked requests while you develop.

FAQ

Can I change the search engine or theme?

Yes. In Settings you can pick Google, DuckDuckGo, Bing, Brave Search, Perplexity or Ecosia, and choose System, Light or Dark.

Is Valkyrie free?

Yes. It's free and open source.

Is there an Android or iPhone app?

Not yet. Valkyrie is a desktop browser for macOS and Windows. A mobile version would be a separate app.

Does Valkyrie send my browsing data anywhere?

No. There are no accounts or analytics. Ad blocking downloads filter lists, and extensions update from the Chrome Web Store, just like in Chrome.

Why does my computer warn me when I open it?

The installers aren't signed with a paid developer certificate yet. See First launch warnings for the one-time fix.

Can I use my Chrome extensions?

Yes, install them from the Chrome Web Store, or import them from Chrome in Settings.
